Used GPU Buying Checklist

A second-hand graphics card can be valuable, but the condition and history matter as much as the model.

Ask for proof of operation

A seller should be able to demonstrate that the card outputs video and reaches normal performance under load.

Inspect the physical condition

Look for damaged connectors, bent brackets, missing screws, corrosion and signs of careless repair.

Check temperatures and clocks

A short stress test or gaming session can expose severe thermal problems or unstable clocks.

Confirm warranty and return terms

A lower price is less attractive when you have no practical protection after purchase.

PCDealHub principle: Buy for your actual workload and verify the listing, seller, price and terms before purchasing.
